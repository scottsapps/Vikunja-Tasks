Getting Started

Veyrn is a native app for Vikunja, the open-source, self-hosted task manager. To use Veyrn you need a Vikunja server: either one you host yourself, or an account on Vikunja Cloud.

To connect to your Vikunja server:

  1. In Vikunja's web interface, go to Settings → API Tokens and create a new token (Veyrn needs at least read and write access to tasks, projects, and labels, but full access is recommended).
  2. Set your expiration date (remember that the token — and Veyrn — will stop working as of that date).
  3. Once you have the API token, enter your server's address and token in Veyrn.
  4. Give the account a short name (useful once you have more than one).

Veyrn talks directly to your Vikunja server — there's no developer-run backend in between, and nothing about your tasks passes through anything ScottsApps operates.

Adding Tasks

Quick Add

Quick Add is the fastest way to get a task in — type it the way you'd say it, and Veyrn picks the due date, project, labels, priority, and repeat out of the sentence as you type, showing a live preview of what it parsed.

  • On iPhone/iPad: tap + from any list, or the Quick Add row.
  • On Mac: press ⌃Space (customizable in Settings) from anywhere on your Mac, or ⌘N from inside Veyrn. The global shortcut opens a small floating panel without switching away from whatever app you were using.

What Quick Add understands, typed anywhere in the text:

Type thisTo set
*labelA label (matches an existing label by prefix, or creates a new one)
+projectThe destination project (matches an existing project by prefix)
!1 – !5Priority, 1 (lowest) to 5 (highest, "DO NOW")
today, tomorrow/tom, tonightA due date
monday, tue, next friday, etc.The next occurrence of that weekday
apr 30, 30 april, apr 30 2027A specific date
2026-09-15, 9/15, 15.9.26A specific date (numeric)
in 3 days, in two weeks, in 1 monthA relative date
next week, next month, end of monthA relative date
every day, daily, every 2 weeks, every mondayA repeat interval

For example, typing Renew passport +Errands *urgent !4 in 3 weeks creates a task titled "Renew passport" in the Errands project, tagged urgent, priority 4, due in three weeks — none of that text stays in the title.

Tap the chevron to expand Quick Add into full chips-and-cards mode, where you can also add a reminder, a repeat rule, or subtasks before saving (subtasks require being online, since each one needs to be created on the server).

Bulk Import

To add a batch of tasks at once — from Settings, or the toolbar's import option — prepare a plain text file shaped like this:

Project Name
label one, label two
2026-10-01 First task title
2026-10-03 Second task title
2026-10-15 Third task title
  • Line 1 is the project the tasks go into (created if it doesn't exist).
  • Line 2 is a comma-separated list of labels applied to every imported task (leave it blank if you don't want any).
  • Every line after that is one task: a date in yyyy-mm-dd format, a space, then the title.

Lines that don't match that shape are skipped and reported back to you rather than silently dropped.

Organizing Tasks

  • Inbox — tasks with no project, the default landing spot.
  • Scheduled — every task with a due date, grouped by day (overdue tasks fold into "Today"). If you've turned on the calendar feature, your calendar events for the day appear here too, above your tasks.
  • Logbook — your completed tasks, searchable.
  • Projects — your own projects, which can be nested inside one another. Tap the disclosure chevron next to a project to expand or collapse its children; a collapsed project's badge rolls up the task count from everything inside it.

Labels and priority are visible at a glance on every task row, along with a subtask progress badge (e.g. "2/5") and a bell icon when a reminder is set.

You can choose how tasks are sorted (alphabetical, priority, or by project) and how projects themselves are ordered, in Settings.

The Task Editor

Tap any task to open its editor. From there you can change:

  • Notes — a rich-text field (bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, headings, lists, links, code) — tap the "Aa" button to reveal formatting controls.
  • Subtasks — break a task into steps; tapping a subtask row opens its own editor. Progress shows on the parent task's row.
  • Due date, reminder, and repeat — set independently; the reminder and due-date pickers are separate so you can be reminded ahead of, not just at, the due date.
  • Priority and labels — as chips you can add or remove.
  • Project — move the task to a different project.

Changes save automatically as you edit; press ⌘↩ on Mac to save and close, or Esc to cancel.

Reminders & Notifications

Set a reminder on any task and Veyrn schedules a local notification for it. From the notification itself you can tap Complete to finish the task or Snooze to push it back an hour, without opening the app.

Completing a task from Veyrn on any of your devices cancels its reminder everywhere — you won't get a notification on your phone for something you already finished on your Mac (a brief internet round-trip may be involved, so this can take a few seconds).

Calendar

Veyrn can optionally show your device's calendar events alongside your tasks — read-only, nothing is created or modified. Turn it on in Settings → Calendar, then choose which calendars to include.

Once enabled, that day's events appear above your tasks in the Scheduled list (you can turn this off separately from the feature itself, if you just want the calendar widget without cluttering Scheduled). A calendar prompt for access to your calendars only appears after you flip the switch on — never before.

Widgets

Veyrn ships two widgets:

  • Veyrn Tasks — your upcoming tasks, grouped by day. Available as a Home Screen widget (medium and large) on iPhone/iPad, Lock Screen and StandBy accessories on iPhone, and a large widget on Mac. Tap a task's circle right on the widget to check it off — no need to open the app.
  • Veyrn Calendar — your day's events and tasks together, in the same sizes.

Widgets refresh periodically on their own; if you've just made a change and want the widget to catch up immediately, use Refresh Widget (the icon with two curved arrows) rather than a plain pull-to-refresh, which updates the app but doesn't force the widget to redraw right away.

Apple Watch

Veyrn's Watch app mirrors whichever account is active on your phone — there's no separate sign-in. It has two views, Scheduled and Inbox, both pull-to-refresh, and a + button to add a task by dictation or Scribble (typed the same natural-language way as Quick Add on the phone).

Add a Smart Stack complication for an at-a-glance count of today's tasks that updates without opening the Watch app; tapping it jumps straight to the Scheduled list.

Working Offline

Veyrn keeps working when you lose your connection. Changes you make offline — completing a task, editing a note, creating a new one — are queued and sent automatically once you're back online.

A pill near the top of the app shows your status: Offline, Reconnecting…, or N pending when changes are queued. Tap the pending pill to see exactly what's waiting to sync, and to discard any change you no longer want sent (for example, if you changed your mind about a task you created offline).

Multiple Accounts

Veyrn supports up to five Vikunja accounts — handy if you have both a personal and a work server, or multiple Vikunja Cloud accounts. Add and switch between them from Settings → Accounts. Switching accounts is a full swap: tasks, projects, and the offline queue are all account-specific, and nothing from one account leaks into another.

Settings

  • Accounts — add, edit, remove, and switch accounts.
  • Tasks — task sort order, project order, font size, and (Mac) the Quick Add keyboard shortcut.
  • Calendar — the calendar feature switch, which calendars to show, and whether events also appear in Scheduled.
  • Opening Page (iPhone only) — which screen Veyrn shows when you return to it: Main, Scheduled, Last Used, or a specific project.
  • Privacy — an optional, anonymous usage-analytics toggle (off doesn't affect anything else about the app).

Search

Search from any list — the Logbook's search runs on the server when your Vikunja version supports it, so you can find completed tasks from any time, not just what's cached locally.

Reporting a Problem

If something looks wrong, use Report a Bug in Settings. It attaches a privacy-scrubbed diagnostic log (no server address, token, or task content — just what happened and when) that makes tracking down the issue much faster. You can view the log yourself before sending, or send without it.
